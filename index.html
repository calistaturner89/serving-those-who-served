Why this resource exists

Care should affirm your whole identity.

Veterans who are LGBTQ+ may face barriers related to discrimination, minority stress, military culture, and the difficulty of finding a provider who understands both experiences. This hub brings community-based options together while clearly identifying what providers publicly document—and what still needs to be confirmed.

How we describe providers: “Both experiences documented” means public professional materials explicitly mention military/veteran work and LGBTQ+ populations. It is not an independent credentialing, safety, or quality endorsement. Availability and payment information should always be confirmed directly.

From resources to change

Building better systems of care

This project advocates for a community mental health system where LGBTQ+ veterans have genuine choices—not just referrals that may or may not be affirming.

01 · Train

Expand LGBTQ+-affirming and military cultural competency training.

02 · Partner

Strengthen referrals between community clinics, LGBTQ+ organizations, and veteran services.

03 · Listen

Include queer veterans in community listening sessions and service design.

04 · Expand

Improve access to affirming trauma care, peer support, and affordable services.

The issue is not whether queer veterans want help. The issue is whether affirming, culturally responsive care is available when they ask for it.

Explore the Southern Colorado LGBTQ+ Needs Assessment and Colorado Health Institute's LGBTQ+ health research for context. No unverified percentages are presented here.
